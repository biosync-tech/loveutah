Neighborhood Focus

Six cities moving right now

Where the data points — and why it matters for your search.

Eagle Mountain

Utah County · New-build market

Median $519,206
YoY +4.3%

Eagle Mountain ranked as one of the fastest-growing cities in the entire US for multiple years. The market has shifted: new-build oversupply means ~567 active listings and a more balanced environment. The draw remains: newer construction, lower entry price vs Lehi/Draper, and a young demographic profile bringing schools and retail. With supply up, buyers now have negotiating room — a sharp change from 2024–2025.

Saratoga Springs

Utah County · Buyer-leaning market

Median $527,450
Supply ~6 mo

At ~6 months of supply and ~627 active listings, Saratoga Springs is the most buyer-friendly market on the northern Wasatch Front. Highridge subdivision listings are sitting at ~199 days on market. This is a genuine shift: sellers are negotiating, contingencies are back on the table, and inspection periods matter again. If you want time to think and room to negotiate, Saratoga Springs delivers that in August 2026.

Lehi

Utah County · Silicon Slopes tech hub

Median $625,000
YoY −0.4%

Lehi sits at the heart of Silicon Slopes — Adobe, Xactware, Qualtrics (despite recent cuts), and dozens of tech employers anchor the city. At $625K (July all-types) and $689K (SF July), it remains one of Utah's most expensive suburbs. The slight YoY decline (−0.4%) reflects inventory normalization, not employer exodus. Single-family in 84043 hit $664,990 in Q2. ~444 active listings, ~5 months supply. Alpine District schools (8/10) keep families paying up.

St. George

Washington County · Southern Utah

Median $517,500
Supply ~5.2 mo

St. George was the fastest-growing metro in the US in 2021–2022, and remote-worker migration from California shows no sign of stopping. Washington County sits at ~$544,990 median, ~5.21 months supply. Inventory is looser than SLC but tighter than Denver. The trade: 300+ days of sunshine, red-rock desert scenery, and more negotiating room than the Wasatch Front. Best option if you want warm weather, outdoor access, and a timeline that doesn't force waived contingencies.

South Jordan

Salt Lake County · Established market

SF Median (84095) $925,000
Q2 2026 KSL

South Jordan sits at the convergence of two major employment corridors — the Bangerter Highway tech strip and the I-15 finance hub from Sandy to Draper. Employers like Goldman Sachs and eBay pull from South Jordan ZIP codes. Single-family in 84095 hit $925,000 in Q2 — a sharp increase reflecting east-side Canyons District demand. The city has mature infrastructure: schools (Canyons 8/10, Corner Canyon HS 9/10), retail, parks. Stability over speculation.

Draper

Salt Lake County · The Point development

SF Median (84020) $925,000
Q2 2026 KSL

Draper anchors the I-15 tech/finance corridor — 1-800 Contacts, Fidelity, eBay, and dozens of mid-market software companies. Single-family in 84020 reached $925,000 in Q2. The Point development (Phase 1 underway, for-sale vertical ~2028) will reshape the city's density and transit profile over the next decade. Canyons District schools (8/10), Corner Canyon outdoor access, and I-15 corridor employment make Draper one of Utah's most stable long-term markets.

Buyer's Guide

Utah First-Time Buyer Programs

Money you may not know about — active programs as of August 2026

UHC FirstHome Loan

Up to 6%

Utah Housing Corp's flagship loan pairs a competitive 30-year rate with down-payment and closing-cost assistance in one package. The most accessible entry point for most first-time buyers.

Min 620 FICO · Income limits apply by county

UHC Down Payment Assist

Up to $25,000

A second mortgage covering your down payment and closing costs. 30-year term at 1% above your first mortgage rate — no balloon payment, no large upfront obligation.

Min 620 FICO · Structured as second mortgage

Utah S.B. 240 New Construction

Up to $20,000

A legislature-funded grant for new builds — applicable to down payment, closing costs, or a permanent interest-rate buydown. Effective for buyers targeting Eagle Mountain or Saratoga Springs where new construction is abundant.

New construction only · First-time buyer required

CDCU Salt Lake City

Deferred loan

Community Development Corporation of Utah. Deferred loan with zero monthly payments and zero interest — for low-to-moderate-income buyers in SLC, Salt Lake County, and Taylorsville. Forgiven after meeting residency requirements.

Income limits · SLC metro only · Forgiven after residency

Law Enforcement Grant

Up to $25,000

A grant — not a loan, no repayment — for active law-enforcement officers using a Utah Housing mortgage. Equals up to 3.5% of the purchase price. Quietly one of the most overlooked programs available.

Active law enforcement · UHC loan required

This Month

August 2026 Market Context

Mortgage Lock-In Effect

Gardner/Wood Jan 2026 forecast: Utah's housing market is "running in place." Approximately 61% of Utah mortgages are locked in under 4%, creating significant inventory constraints as homeowners resist trading up to 6.65% rates.

Source: KUER, January 2026

Rental Market Shift

Utah County rents down ~−5.6% YoY with +30% more rental listings (Realtor.com county-level data). As for-sale inventory normalizes and rates stabilize, some would-be buyers are opting to rent and wait.

Source: Realtor.com, 2026

Qualtrics Cuts

Qualtrics announced layoffs following the $6.75B acquisition by Press Ganey + Forsta. Utah-specific impact undisclosed. Lehi tech employment remains strong overall, but individual employer volatility continues.

Source: Tech press, 2026

Data Center Construction

Gardner report April 2026: 48 data centers operational in Utah. Construction peaked ~2025; modest operations jobs created; water stress concerns remain in drought-constrained regions.

Source: Gardner Institute, April 2026

The Point (Draper)

Phase 1 underway. For-sale vertical housing expected ~2028. Will reshape Draper's density and transit profile over the next decade as a major mixed-use development on former prison land.

Source: Draper development updates, 2026

SB 284 ADU Law

Effective October 1, 2026: detached ADUs allowed on lots ≥11,000 sq ft. Cities may keep owner-occupancy requirements. Could gradually increase housing supply in established neighborhoods.

Source: Utah Legislature, 2026
